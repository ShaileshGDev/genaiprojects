Here’s the current jargon in simple terms, especially in the 2025–26 AI-search world: [digiday](https://digiday.com/media/wtf-are-geo-and-aeo-and-how-they-differ-from-seo/)

## SEO (Search Engine Optimization)

- Goal: Help your pages rank in traditional search engines (Google, Bing) so people click through to your site. [within](https://www.within.co/blog/generative-engine-optimization/)
- Focus:  
  - Keywords and search intent  
  - On‑page content (titles, headings, body, internal links)  
  - Technical SEO (crawlability, speed, mobile, structured data)  
  - Backlinks and overall authority  

Think of SEO as: “How do I get my page in the top organic results so users click to my website?” [digitalmarketinginstitute](https://digitalmarketinginstitute.com/blog/what-is-seo-geo-and-aeo)

## AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)

- Goal: Make your content the thing that gets pulled into direct answers – featured snippets, “People Also Ask”, voice assistants (Alexa, Google Assistant), AI answer boxes, etc. [within](https://www.within.co/blog/generative-engine-optimization/)
- Focus:  
  - Question–answer style content (“What is…”, “How to…”, “Best way to…”)  
  - Very clear, concise answers and step lists  
  - Structured data / FAQ schema so machines can parse answers easily  

Think of AEO as: “How do I become *the* short, direct answer a user hears or sees, even if they never click my site?” [digitalmarketinginstitute](https://digitalmarketinginstitute.com/blog/what-is-seo-geo-and-aeo)

## GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)

- Goal: Get your brand/content cited inside AI-generated answers from LLM-based tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Google SGE/AI Overviews). [jasper](https://www.jasper.ai/blog/geo-aeo)
- Focus:  
  - High‑quality, in‑depth, well-structured content that LLMs can trust  
  - Strong signals of expertise, authoritativeness, trust (E‑E‑A‑T)  
  - Off‑site signals: mentions, links, discussions on forums like Reddit/Quora  
  - Content phrased in natural, conversational language that matches how people prompt AI tools  

Think of GEO as: “How do I get AI assistants to *use and cite* my content when they compose multi-paragraph answers?” [jasper](https://www.jasper.ai/blog/geo-aeo)

## How they relate (quick comparison)

All three share the same ultimate aim: visibility. The differences are *where* you’re trying to show up and *how* users interact: [within](https://www.within.co/blog/generative-engine-optimization/)

- SEO → visible in search result lists.  
- AEO → visible as the direct, atomic answer.  
- GEO → visible as a cited source in AI-generated, multi-sentence responses.

For someone technical like you, a useful mental model is:

- SEO = optimize for ranked documents.  
- AEO = optimize for extracted answer spans.  
- GEO = optimize for being part of an LLM’s high‑confidence knowledge graph.

## Step 1: Create static landing pages (no search feature needed)

Goal: Have one crawlable URL per **city + area + category** that lists relevant profiles and explains the topic. [uberall](https://uberall.com/en-us/resources/blog/how-to-create-content-for-local-landing-pages-for-seo)

For example:

- URL: `/mumbai/andheri-east/civil-contractors/`

Implementation idea (framework‑agnostic):

1. Add a new page type in your app (e.g., `LocationCategoryPage`).  
2. Store mapping like:  
   - city: `mumbai`  
   - area: `andheri-east`  
   - category: `civil-contractors`.  
3. On that page, **server-side render** a list of profiles that match this combination (from your DB). No search box needed; it’s just a static listing. [wpzinc](https://www.wpzinc.com/local-seo-landing-pages-wordpress/)

From a user’s perspective, it’s a directory page. From your perspective, it’s just another route that pre-filters data.

## Step 2: Design the content of that landing page

You don’t need to overthink design; copy patterns used in other local directories, but simpler. [bluleadz](https://www.bluleadz.com/blog/location-pages-and-local-seo)

Basic structure:

1. **SEO/LLM-friendly header**

   - `<title>`: “Civil Contractors in Andheri East, Mumbai | [Brand]”.  
   - `<h1>`: “Civil Contractors in Andheri East, Mumbai”.

2. **Short answer block at the top (AEO/GEO)**

   One small paragraph that clearly answers: “Who are the civil contractors in Andheri East?” and says your platform lists verified profiles with location, services, etc. [neilpatel](https://neilpatel.com/blog/local-seo-llms/)

3. **List of contractors (static)**

   - Label like “Civil contractor in Andheri East, Mumbai”.  
   - 1–2 line description (e.g., “Residential and commercial renovation”).  
   - Maybe rating / years of experience if you store it.

4. **Helpful local content block**

## Step 3: Connect landing page and profiles (without encouraging hopping)

You don’t want users to jump away once they’re on a contractor’s profile. That’s fine.

Do this:

- On the **landing page**, link each contractor name to its profile.  
- On the **profile**, do NOT show “other contractors” or a search box. Just show the contractor’s own details and your desired conversion action (WhatsApp, call, etc.).  

For SEO/AI, what matters is that:

- Landing page → links out to profiles.  
- Profiles → mention consistent location and category text (“Civil contractor in Andheri East, Mumbai”) so engines see them as part of that cluster. [neilpatel](https://neilpatel.com/blog/local-seo-llms/)

You can optionally have a very small breadcrumb on the profile, like:

- “Home > Mumbai > Andheri East > Civil contractors”

where only “Home” and maybe “Andheri East” are clickable, but **not** an obvious directory of competitors. That still helps search engines understand hierarchy without pushing users to hop. [brightlocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/location-pages/)
